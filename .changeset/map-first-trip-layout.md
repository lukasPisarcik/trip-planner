The trip page is now map-first. On any trip with coordinates (and a Maps key) the live map fills the whole viewport on every tab and never collapses or scrolls away: it runs as a fixed canvas under a frosted-glass sidebar (the map shows through it), a title-only glass card sits top-left, and the tabs plus their content move into one right-hand glass panel (440px at ≥1280px, 400px on tablets) whose frosted tab strip stays pinned while only the body below it scrolls — the page and the map stay put. On phones the same panel docks as a bottom sheet over the visible map. The day legend is gone because every itinerary card now carries its route colour (number badge + a 3px left stripe), and a marker or route click still reveals the panel, switches to Itinerary and scrolls that day's card into view inside it. A chevron on the panel edge slides it off-screen (a "Show panel" button or Escape brings it back, and the map refits to the uncovered area both ways), replacing the old expand-map button and scrim, which are meaningless now that the map is always full-screen. Every fit pads past the header, title card, sidebar (at its current width), panel or sheet and an open AI panel, via a small unit-tested geometry helper, so focused stops are never hidden under chrome; Google's own zoom control is off (every corner sits under a card) in favour of ± buttons in the map's control cluster, and gestures are always greedy since nothing else on the page scrolls. Inside that panel every card is now see-through, outlined only by its hairline border and route stripe so the map shows through the whole sheet, and the AI assistant drawer is a floating frosted-glass card on the map canvas with the same insets, radius and material as the content panel. Trips without a map keep the solid banner, sticky tab bar and scrolling page unchanged.
